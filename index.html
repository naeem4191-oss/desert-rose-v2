A desert rose takes thousands of years to grow. Scroll to watch one rise from the sand.

A rose that grows without water.

Beneath Qatar's sabkha, the coastal salt flats, groundwater rises and evaporates. Gypsum or baryte crystallises in thin blades around grains of sand, locking together into petals over millennia.

Gypsum · baryteSabkha salt flatsMillennia

The rose, made to keep.

No two desert roses are alike, and the natural crystal is fragile. This sculpture holds its form in resin: every blade, every fold.

Every petal, preserved.

Ten centimetres across and 200 grams, small enough to hold in one hand.

Look closer.

A rose the size of a city.

Architect Jean Nouvel drew on the desert rose for the National Museum of Qatar: great curved discs, interlocking like petals, wrapped around the historic palace of Sheikh Abdullah bin Jassim Al Thani.
